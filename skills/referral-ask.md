# Skill: referral-ask

## Trigger

Invoked when a rep wants to ask a closed-won client for a referral, introduction, or testimonial.

---

## Inputs Required

Four inputs. Ask for any that are missing.

1. **Client name** — the company
2. **What was sold** — one sentence on the product or service
3. **Outcome achieved so far** — the specific, measurable result the client has seen (revenue added, time saved, ramp compressed, pipeline built — in their terms if possible)
4. **Buyer's name and role** — the person who signed or championed the deal

---

## Output

Two referral request messages. Ready to send. Both anchor to the outcome, not the relationship or the favor.

---

## Core Rule

A referral ask that leads with the relationship ("we've had such a great experience working together") sounds transactional even when it tries not to. A referral ask that leads with the outcome the client achieved makes the ask feel natural — you're giving them something specific to talk about when they make the introduction.

The messages do not ask for "anyone you know." They ask for a specific type of company or person — narrow enough to make it easy to think of one name.

---

## Writing Rules

**Banned words and phrases:**
leverage, synergies, robust, cutting-edge, delve, seamless, I wanted to reach out, hope this finds you well, I'd love to, pick your brain, as we discussed, it's been a pleasure, I truly appreciate, means the world, touch base, circle back, anyone who might benefit, mutual connections, I know you're busy.

**Tone:** Peer to peer. Direct. The rep is sharing a result and making a specific, easy ask — not requesting a favor.

**The ask structure:**
1. Name the outcome (specific number or result — from the input)
2. State who else might be in a similar position (draws from the ICP or the client's network context)
3. One specific ask — an introduction, a name, or a LinkedIn connection to a specific type of person
4. Make it frictionless — tell them exactly what you'll do once they connect you

**Never:**
- Ask for "any referrals" (too vague — makes the reader work)
- Lead with the relationship before the outcome
- Use language that implies they owe the rep something
- Make the ask feel like a performance review

---

## Quality Check (Run Before Delivering)

- [ ] LinkedIn message is under 75 words
- [ ] Email is under 100 words
- [ ] Both lead with the outcome, not the relationship
- [ ] Both make a specific ask, not a general "anyone you know"
- [ ] No banned words
- [ ] Tone is peer-to-peer, not grateful-vendor
- [ ] Both tell the buyer exactly what happens after they say yes

---

## Output Format

Deliver with no preamble. Use this exact structure:

---

**Referral Ask — [Client Name] | [Buyer Name]**

---

**LinkedIn Message**
[body — under 75 words]
*Word count: [n]/75*

---

**Email**

[body — under 100 words]

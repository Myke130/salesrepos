# Skill: discovery-call-prep

## Trigger

Invoked when the user needs to prep for a discovery call, wants structured call questions, or asks for a talk track before meeting with a prospect.

---

## Inputs Required

Three inputs. Ask for any that are missing before proceeding.

1. **Company name** — the prospect company
2. **Contact role** — title or department (e.g. "VP of Sales," "Head of RevOps")
3. **What the rep's client sells** — one sentence

If the user has already run `/prospect-research`, ask them to paste the brief — it will make every output sharper. If not, proceed with the three inputs above.

---

## Output

A single structured call prep sheet. Target read time: under 5 minutes. Designed for a 30-minute discovery call.

---

## Writing Rules

**Banned words and phrases:**
leverage, synergies, robust, cutting-edge, delve, seamless, streamline, empower, game-changer, transformative, innovative, circle back, touch base, excited to connect, at the end of the day, move the needle, best-in-class, value-add, pain points (use "friction" or name the specific problem), solution (use the specific product category instead).

**Tone:** Direct prep notes — written like a colleague briefing you 10 minutes before you get on the call. No corporate padding. Every line has to earn its place.

**Specificity rule:** Every question and objection response must be specific to this contact's role and company situation. No generic discovery questions that could apply to any call.

---

## Process

1. If a prospect-research brief was provided, extract the company's current situation, pain signals, and contact context from it.
2. If only the three raw inputs were provided, reason from first principles: what is a person in this role typically responsible for, what friction does that role face, and how does what the rep's client sells address it?
3. Build the four sections below.

---

## Output Format

Deliver the full sheet with no preamble. Use this structure exactly:

---

### Discovery Call Prep — [Company Name]
**Contact:** [Role]
**What you're selling:** [one sentence]
**Call duration:** 30 minutes

---

#### Talk Track Opening (60–90 seconds)

One paragraph. This is what the rep says in the first 90 seconds to frame the call, establish credibility without pitching, and hand control to the prospect with a question.

Structure:
- Acknowledge why you're here (brief, not flattering)
- One specific observation about the company or role that shows you did homework
- Frame what you want to get out of the call
- Open question to hand them the floor

No pitching in the opener. No product features. No "I'd love to tell you about."

---

#### 5 Discovery Questions

Each question must:
- Be specific to this company's situation or this contact's role — not a generic discovery template
- Have a clear intent (labeled in brackets after each question): [Uncover current process] / [Identify friction] / [Qualify urgency] / [Map stakeholders] / [Test for fit]
- Be open-ended — no yes/no questions

Format each as:
**Q[number]:** [Question]
*Intent: [label] — [one sentence on why this question matters for this specific call]*

---

#### 3 Likely Objections with Scripted Responses

Based on the contact's role, company stage, and what is being sold — identify the three objections most likely to come up in the first 30 minutes.

Format each as:

**Objection:** "[Likely exact wording]"
**Why this comes up:** [One sentence — specific to their situation]
**Response:** [2–3 sentences. Acknowledge the reality behind the objection before addressing it. No dismissing. No pivoting immediately to a feature.]

---

#### One Trap to Avoid

One specific behavioral or conversational mistake that would derail this particular call — given what you know about this company, this role, and what's being sold.

Not generic advice. Name the trap, explain why it's a trap here specifically, and give one sentence on what to do instead.

Format:
**The trap:** [Name it]
**Why it kills this call:** [One sentence]
**Instead:** [One sentence]

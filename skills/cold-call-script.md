# Skill: cold-call-script

## Trigger

Invoked when a rep needs a cold call opener, a phone script for first-touch outreach, or a scripted response to early call objections.

---

## Inputs Required

Two inputs. Ask for any that are missing.

1. **Account brief** — the completed 6-section brief from `/prospect-research`
2. **Contact's role** — title and department (can come from the brief)

If no account brief is available, ask for: company name, one recent company signal, contact role, and what the rep's client sells.

---

## Output

A 90-second cold call opener. Four components. Written to be spoken, not read — every line must sound natural when said aloud.

---

## The Core Rule

This script must never sound scripted. The test: if a rep reads it word-for-word and it sounds like they're reading, rewrite it. Short sentences. Natural pauses built in. No corporate language. No feature lists. No pitch in the opener.

---

## Writing Rules

**Banned words and phrases:**
leverage, synergies, robust, cutting-edge, delve, seamless, reach out, touch base, circle back, I was hoping to connect, the reason for my call today is, I wanted to introduce, we help companies like yours, I know you're busy, I'll be brief, is now a bad time (use a better permission ask), pain points (name the specific problem), solution (name the category).

**Spoken language rules:**
- Contractions throughout ("we've" not "we have," "you're" not "you are")
- Sentences under 15 words where possible
- No lists — lists don't work on phone calls
- One idea per sentence
- The word "actually" and "honestly" are permitted — they sound human

**Pattern interrupt rule:**
The opening line must break the expected cold call pattern. It is not an introduction, not a permission ask, not a compliment. It is a statement or observation that causes the prospect to pause and listen. It references something specific to this company or this person — not a generic category observation.

---

## Component 1: Pattern Interrupt Opening Line

One sentence. Said before the rep introduces themselves.

It references a specific, real finding from the account brief — a product launch, a hire, a funding round, a job posting signal, or something the prospect has said publicly.

It is not a question. It is a statement that earns the next 10 seconds.

Examples of the type (not the actual lines — generate from the brief):
- "Saw you just crossed $500M ARR — and you've got 95 open roles on Greenhouse right now."
- "Your Mission Andromeda launch in February was the first time I've seen a revenue tool company build onboarding for their own product."

After this line, the rep introduces themselves: "[Name], I work with [client company / what they do, one phrase]."

---

## Component 2: Company-Specific Observation

One to two sentences. Connects the opening finding to a tension or gap — something that creates a question in the prospect's mind without stating the answer.

This is not a pitch. It is an observation that signals the rep understands their world. It ends with an open space — the prospect should feel the relevance before the rep asks anything.

---

## Component 3: Curiosity Question

One question. It does not pitch. It does not explain the product. It creates curiosity by asking about the prospect's current experience with the problem area.

It is phrased to feel like a genuine inquiry, not a setup for a pitch. The prospect should feel safe answering it.

Never: "Would you be interested in learning how we can help you with X?"
Instead: Something that asks about their current state, their experience, or their thinking — in a way that opens a conversation.

---

## Component 4: Objection Responses

Two scripted responses. Written to be spoken. Natural, not defensive.

**"Not interested"**
Do not argue. Do not ask why. Use a disarming acknowledgment, then pivot with one specific observation or question that earns 30 more seconds. If they repeat "not interested," respect it and end cleanly.

**"Send me an email"**
Do not say "sure, will do." Use a brief acknowledgment, then ask one question that either qualifies them (worth sending) or surfaces whether the email is a brush-off. If they confirm they want the email, commit to a specific send time.

---

## Full Script Format

Deliver the full script written as it would be spoken — in sequence, with natural flow. Label each component in brackets so the rep knows what they're doing, but the labels do not replace the content.

Use this format:

---

### Cold Call Script — [Company Name] | [Contact Role]

**[Pattern Interrupt + Intro]**
[line]

**[Observation]**
[lines]

**[Curiosity Question]**
[question]

---

**If "Not Interested":**
[response]

---

**If "Send Me an Email":**
[response]

---

**Delivery note:** [One sentence on tone — e.g., "Say the opening line before they expect the call to start — calm, unhurried, like you're sharing something you noticed."]

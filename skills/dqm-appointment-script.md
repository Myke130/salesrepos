# Skill: dqm-appointment-script

## Trigger

Invoked when a rep needs a structured appointment script using the Diagnostic Questioning Method (DQM) — for any stage of the sales process.

---

## What DQM Is

DQM is a question-based sales methodology built on the principle that people are more persuaded by conclusions they reach themselves than conclusions pitched at them. The rep's role is to ask questions that surface the prospect's own awareness of their problem, the consequences of leaving it unsolved, and what an ideal outcome looks like — in that order.

The technique is not manipulative. It is precise listening structured as a conversation. The prospect does most of the talking. The rep guides.

The output of this skill is a script that sounds like a real conversation — not a questionnaire. Questions are written to be asked once, naturally, and build on each other.

---

## Inputs Required

Three inputs. Ask for any that are missing.

1. **Account brief** — the completed 6-section brief from `/prospect-research`
2. **Contact's role** — title and department
3. **Appointment type** — one of: discovery, demo, follow-up, or closing call

---

## Output

A full DQM-structured appointment script with six sections. Every question must be specific to this company and contact — derived from the brief, not generic DQM templates.

---

## Writing Rules

**Banned words and phrases:**
leverage, synergies, robust, cutting-edge, delve, seamless, streamline, empower, game-changer, transformative, as I mentioned, I'd like to share, I think you'll find, the reason I'm asking is, does that make sense, fair enough, absolutely, totally, for sure, pain points (name the specific issue), solution (name the category).

**Tone rule:** DQM lives and dies on tonality. The questions are secondary — how they're delivered is primary. Each section has a required tone note. The rep must internalize the tone before speaking the question, not perform it.

**Specificity rule:** Every question in this script references something specific to this company, this contact's role, or this industry. No question should be usable in a script for a different company without modification.

**Question design rules:**
- Questions are open-ended — never yes/no
- Each question builds logically from the previous answer
- No question contains the answer ("Don't you think it would be better if...")
- No loaded questions ("What's the biggest challenge you're facing today?" is too broad — name the specific area)
- Transition phrases between sections sound natural, not mechanical

---

## DQM Script Structure

### Section 1: Connection Statement

**Purpose:** Establish why you're here, frame the conversation as a mutual exploration, and lower the prospect's guard before any questions begin.

**Tone:** Unhurried, neutral, slightly curious. You are not here to sell. You are here to understand whether there's a fit worth discussing.

**Structure:**
- Acknowledge the appointment briefly (one sentence)
- Reference one specific thing from the brief that shows preparation
- Frame the session: what you hope to understand, and that the prospect should feel free to be direct if it's not relevant
- Hand the floor to them with a soft opener question

Length: 4–6 sentences max.

---

### Section 2: Situation Questions (3–4 questions)

**Purpose:** Understand the prospect's current state — processes, tools, team structure, recent changes. These questions gather facts and signal that the rep has done their homework.

**Tone:** Genuinely curious, conversational. These feel like a peer asking about how things work — not an intake form.

**Design:** Questions in this section should surface the current state that makes the problem possible. They set up Section 3 without telegraphing where the conversation is going.

Format each as:
**S[number]:** [Question]
*Tonality: [one sentence on delivery — e.g., "Ask this like you're genuinely curious, not confirming what you already know."]*

---

### Section 3: Problem Awareness Questions (3–4 questions)

**Purpose:** Help the prospect identify and articulate a problem they may have normalized. These questions do not name the problem — they guide the prospect to name it themselves.

**Tone:** Empathetic, unhurried. Slightly more serious than Section 2. The rep is sitting with the prospect in the problem, not pointing at it.

**Design:** Questions build from the situation facts. They ask about friction, gaps, or consequences within the current state the prospect just described. The prospect should feel understood, not interrogated.

Format each as:
**P[number]:** [Question]
*Tonality: [one sentence on delivery]*

---

### Section 4: Consequence Questions (2–3 questions)

**Purpose:** Amplify the prospect's awareness of what the unsolved problem costs — in time, money, revenue, morale, or opportunity. The prospect names the consequence; the rep does not.

**Tone:** Concerned, measured. The rep is taking the problem seriously on the prospect's behalf. Not alarmist. Grounded.

**Design:** Questions in this section ask what happens when the problem continues — downstream effects, opportunity costs, or compounding friction. Each question should make the status quo feel more expensive than it did before.

Format each as:
**C[number]:** [Question]
*Tonality: [one sentence on delivery]*

---

### Section 5: Solution Questions (2–3 questions)

**Purpose:** Get the prospect to describe what an ideal outcome looks like in their own words. These words become the closing language in Section 6.

**Tone:** Hopeful, forward-looking. The energy shifts here — slightly warmer. The rep is inviting the prospect to imagine a better state.

**Design:** Questions ask what the prospect would want, what success would look like, or what would need to be true for them to feel confident making a change. Do not name your product or service in these questions. Let the prospect describe the outcome, not the vendor.

Format each as:
**Q[number]:** [Question]
*Tonality: [one sentence on delivery]*

---

### Section 6: Commitment Close

**Purpose:** Tie the prospect's own words back to a specific next step. The close uses the language the prospect used in Section 5 — not the rep's language.

**Tone:** Collaborative, calm. Not urgent. Not pushy. The rep is reflecting back what the prospect said and asking if they want to move toward it.

**Structure:**
- Brief recap of what the prospect said they want (1–2 sentences, using their words or close paraphrases)
- One question that connects their stated outcome to a next step
- The specific ask: a meeting, a proposal review, a decision, or a timeline commitment — matched to the appointment type

**Note:** Leave a blank after the recap line in the script so the rep can fill in the prospect's actual words from the call. The template provides the structure; the rep fills in the prospect's language in the moment.

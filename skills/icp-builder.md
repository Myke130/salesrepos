# Skill: icp-builder

## Trigger

Invoked when a rep starts a new client engagement and needs a structured Ideal Customer Profile, or when an existing ICP needs to be rebuilt from new client data.

---

## Inputs Required

Four inputs. Ask for any that are missing.

1. **What they sell** — one to three sentences describing the product or service
2. **Who they've closed before** — company names, industries, sizes, or descriptions of past customers (even rough: "mostly mid-size SaaS companies around 200 employees")
3. **Average deal size** — a number or range
4. **Geography** — where they sell (countries, regions, or "remote/anywhere")

If the client has no closed customers yet (new product, new market), use inputs 1, 3, and 4 to reason from first principles. Note when the ICP is hypothetical vs. derived from actual win data.

---

## Output

A single structured ICP document. This is the rep's reference for every prospecting session on this engagement. Built once, referenced repeatedly.

---

## Writing Rules

**Banned words and phrases:**
leverage, synergies, robust, cutting-edge, delve, seamless, streamline, empower, game-changer, transformative, innovative, world-class, best-in-class, visionary, disrupting, pain points (name the specific problem), solution (use the product category), decision-makers (name the actual title).

**Specificity rule:** Every field in the ICP must be specific enough to use as a filter. "Mid-size companies" is not specific. "50–500 employees, Series B or later, or bootstrapped with $5M+ revenue" is. If the input data doesn't support that level of specificity, say so — do not fabricate precision.

**Source rule:** When firmographic ranges or persona priorities are derived from the closed customer data, note that. When they're inferred, note that too. The rep needs to know what's confirmed vs. hypothetical.

---

## Process

1. Analyze the closed customer list for patterns: industry clusters, company size ranges, growth stage signals, tech stack indicators if mentioned.
2. Derive the firmographic profile from those patterns.
3. Identify trigger events — the circumstances or moments that likely preceded a purchase decision.
4. Build three decision maker personas from the most common buyer titles in this segment.
5. Write the briefing summary last, after all other sections are complete.

---

## Output Format

Deliver with no preamble. Use this exact structure:

---

### Ideal Customer Profile — [Client Name / Product Name]
**Built from:** [n closed deals / hypothetical — note which]
**Date:** [today's date]

---

#### Company Firmographics

| Field | Profile |
|---|---|
| Industry | [specific industries or verticals — list up to 4] |
| Company size | [headcount range] |
| Revenue range | [if inferrable from deal size and segment] |
| Growth stage | [e.g., Series B–D / bootstrapped profitable / enterprise division] |
| Geography | [from input] |
| Tech indicators | [CRM, tools, or infrastructure signals that suggest fit — if applicable] |
| Exclusions | [company types or sizes that don't fit — derived from losses or mismatches if mentioned] |

**Notes on confidence:** [One sentence on which fields are confirmed from win data vs. inferred.]

---

#### Trigger Events

The circumstances, announcements, or internal changes that signal a company is likely to buy. List 4–6. Each trigger should be specific enough to use as a search or monitoring filter.

Format each as:
**Trigger:** [Name of the event or signal]
**Why it signals buying intent:** [One sentence — specific to what the client sells]
**Where to spot it:** [LinkedIn, job boards, press releases, funding announcements, etc.]

---

#### Decision Maker Personas

Three personas. Derived from the most common buyer titles in this segment. If the client has closed deals, use the actual titles from those wins. If not, reason from the product category and company type.

Format each as:

**Persona [number]: [Title]**
**Department:** [Sales / RevOps / HR / Finance / etc.]
**What they're measured on:** [Their KPIs — specific to this role and segment]
**What they care about in a vendor conversation:** [What earns credibility with this person]
**Likely objection:** [The one pushback this persona almost always raises]
**How to reach them:** [Cold email / LinkedIn / referral / event — what works for this title]

---

#### ICP Briefing Summary

One paragraph. Written for the rep to read aloud or silently before a prospecting session. Covers: who the ideal company is, what's happening in their world that creates demand, and which persona to find first. Specific enough to be useful. Short enough to memorize.

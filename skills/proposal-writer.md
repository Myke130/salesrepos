# Skill: proposal-writer

## Trigger

Invoked when a rep needs to write a proposal, pricing document, or one-page engagement summary after a discovery call.

---

## Input Required

The structured CRM entry from `/call-notes-to-crm`. At minimum:
- Summary (what was discussed and what was learned)
- Objections Raised (if any)
- Stakeholders Mentioned
- Deal Stage Recommendation

If a discovery call prep sheet or additional notes are available, accept those too — they add context for the problem statement and pricing rationale.

If the CRM entry does not contain enough information to write a specific problem statement (e.g., only "Discovery" as a stage with no summary), ask the rep to add 2–3 sentences on what the prospect said about their situation before proceeding.

---

## Output

A one-page proposal outline. Under 500 words. Five sections. Written to be sent directly to the prospect or used as the basis for a formal proposal document.

---

## Core Rule

The problem statement must use the prospect's own words or a close paraphrase from the call notes. If the prospect named a specific friction, number, timeline, or consequence — it goes in the problem statement verbatim or near-verbatim. A proposal that describes the prospect's problem in the rep's language, not the prospect's, loses.

---

## Writing Rules

**Banned words and phrases:**
leverage, synergies, robust, cutting-edge, delve, seamless, streamline, empower, game-changer, transformative, innovative, world-class, best-in-class, comprehensive, end-to-end, holistic, full-service, partnership, journey, ecosystem, moving forward, as discussed, per our conversation, I am pleased to present, please find attached, value-add, actionable, impactful.

**Tone:** Direct, plain language. Written as a peer making a specific proposal to another businessperson — not a vendor submitting a bid. Short sentences. No jargon. If a word has a simpler equivalent, use the simpler word.

**Pricing rule:** The three pricing options (Good/Better/Best) must differ in scope, speed, or depth — not just in price. Each option must have a one-line description of what it includes and what it doesn't. Never present pricing without anchoring it to an outcome.

**Numbers rule:** Use specific numbers where available from the CRM entry (deal size, timeline, headcount, revenue target). Where numbers weren't discussed, write around the gap rather than filling it with placeholders like "[INSERT PRICE HERE]" — instead, note that pricing will be confirmed after [specific condition].

---

## Process

1. Extract the prospect's language from the Summary and any Objections Raised in the CRM entry.
2. Write the Problem Statement using that language first.
3. Write the Proposed Solution in plain language — what will be done, by whom, over what timeframe.
4. Build three pricing options that ladder in scope: Good (minimum viable engagement), Better (full scope), Best (accelerated or expanded).
5. Define 2–3 success metrics that the prospect would recognize as meaningful — derived from what they said, not generic KPIs.
6. Write the Next Step as a single, specific action with a clear owner and timeframe.

---

## Pricing Option Structure

Each option needs four lines:

**[Good / Better / Best]**
- What's included: [specific scope, not a feature list]
- What's not included: [one line — sets expectations]
- Investment: [price or "to be confirmed after [condition]"]
- Expected outcome: [one sentence tied to the prospect's stated goal]

---

## Quality Check (Run Before Delivering)

- [ ] Under 500 words total
- [ ] Problem statement uses the prospect's language from the CRM notes
- [ ] No banned words anywhere
- [ ] Three pricing options differ in scope, not just price
- [ ] Each pricing option has an outcome statement
- [ ] Success metrics are specific to this prospect's stated goals
- [ ] Next Step names one action, one owner, and one timeframe
- [ ] No placeholder text — where information is missing, write around it or note the condition for confirmation

Rewrite any section that fails. Re-check after rewriting.

---

## Output Format

Deliver with no preamble. Use this exact structure:

---

**Proposal — [Company Name]**
**Prepared for:** [Contact Name, Title — from CRM entry]
**Date:** [today's date]

---

**The Problem**

[Problem statement in the prospect's own words or close paraphrase. 2–4 sentences. Specific. No interpretation or framing added by the rep — this is the prospect's reality as they described it.]

---

**What We're Proposing**

[Plain language description of the engagement. What will be done, by whom, in what timeframe, and what it will produce. 3–5 sentences. No jargon. No feature lists.]

---

**Options**

**Good — [Option Name]**
[Four-line structure per pricing rule above]

**Better — [Option Name]**
[Four-line structure]

**Best — [Option Name]**
[Four-line structure]

---

**How We'll Know It's Working**

[2–3 specific success metrics — derived from the prospect's stated goals. Format: metric name + what it measures + why it matters for this engagement.]

---

**Next Step**

[Single action. Format: [Action] — owned by [person] — by [date or condition]. One sentence. No ambiguity.]
